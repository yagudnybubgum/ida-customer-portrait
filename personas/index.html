Customer portrait

Three use cases

Country still decides the job. Licence data splits “home use”: Canada is immigrants with foreign licences; UK is foreign-licence heavy but only ~half home destination; IN/US/AU are local-licence trip buyers. True domestic (local licence + home) is rare everywhere.

Immigrant · home

Foreign-licence local driver

Strongest in Canada: lives there, licence issued elsewhere, IDP for local driving — not a trip shopper. UK shares the foreign-licence pattern (~70%) but home destination is only ~54%, so treat UK as mixed, not a Canada clone.

CA 73.7% foreign · 81% home UK 70.4% foreign · 54% home Licence ≠ billing

Why it matters: Canada landings like “Drive legally in Canada with your foreign licence” (GT/MX creative). UK: foreign-licence / diaspora angle, don’t assume every buyer is home-use.

Outbound traveler

The trip buyer

Local licence, foreign destination. Dominant in India, USA, Australia (77–86% licence match).

IN / US / AU Licence ≈ billing Destination ≠ billing

Why it matters: trip timing, destination corridors, classic travel IDP proof points.

Local licence · home

True domestic minority

Licence and destination match billing. Distinct from “licence match” alone (IN/US/AU match is mostly travelers). Minority in every market; never the growth narrative.

Licence = billing Destination = billing Rare everywhere

Why it matters: don’t build CA messaging around “locals getting an IDP for home use,” and don’t confuse local-licence travelers with this segment.

🇨🇦 Canada

LatAm immigrant corridor

Home use 81.1% · foreign licence 73.7% · GT+MX ≈ 79% of foreign

Not a domestic Canadian with a local licence. Mostly arrivals / workers from Guatemala and Mexico, buying an IDP to drive on a foreign licence inside Canada. Cleanest immigrant-home market of the five (home + foreign licence both high).

Immigrant · home Guatemala 58.7% Mexico 20.2%

Message: “Drive legally in Canada with your foreign licence.” Skip trip-planning creative.

🇬🇧 UK

South Asian diaspora corridor

Home use 54.1% · foreign licence 70.4% · India 54.7% of foreign

Foreign-licence heavy like Canada, but not the same job mix: only ~half of destinations are UK. India dominates foreign licences; US / Brazil / Nigeria trail. Without a licence × destination cross-tab, don’t treat every foreign-licence UK order as home driving.

Foreign licence · mixed India 54.7% USA 5.3%

Message: diaspora / foreign-licence creative (India-first). Don’t copy-paste Canada’s pure “drive at home” funnel.

🇺🇸 USA

Europe-bound traveler

Home use 13.3% · foreign licence only 16.7% · 83.3% licence match

Classic trip buyer with a US licence. Demand clusters on European driving destinations.

Outbound traveler Europe focus

🇮🇳 India

Long-haul trip buyer

Home use 6.5% · foreign licence only 14.0% · 86.0% licence match

Most outbound of the five. Local Indian licence, destinations abroad. Also the main licence-origin source into the UK foreign-licence segment.

Outbound traveler Asia + West

Top destinations: Thailand, Indonesia, USA, Italy, UK

🇦🇺 Australia

Asia + Europe trip buyer

Home use 24.9% · foreign licence 23.0% · 77.0% licence match

Travel-first market with mostly local licences. Top clusters span short-haul Asia (Indonesia) and long-haul Europe (Italy) — clearer than CA/UK outbound scatter.

Outbound traveler Indonesia / Italy

💡
Product takeaway

Canada needs an immigrant-compliance funnel, not outbound travel. UK needs a foreign-licence / diaspora funnel without assuming home-use for every order. US / India / Australia stay classic trip buyers. Separate landings and creative by licence-origin corridor where the data is clean (esp. GT/MX → CA, IN → UK).
